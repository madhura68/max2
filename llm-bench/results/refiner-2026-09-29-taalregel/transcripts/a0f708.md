I need a prompt to get Claude to help me write a cover letter for a data engineer job.

### Model

Four questions to sharpen the prompt:

1. Do you have a specific job posting (title, company, requirements) to tailor the letter to, or should it be a general application? [default: you paste the job description into the prompt]
2. What material can Claude draw on about you: your CV, a list of projects, or just a few facts you type in? [default: you paste your CV or a short summary]
3. What style and length do you want? [default: professional but not stiff, about 250–300 words, no clichés like "I am passionate about..."]
4. What should Claude do with the result: write the final letter directly, or draft it and stop for your feedback before finalizing? [default: deliver a complete first draft you can edit]

### Gebruiker

Go with your suggestions. Extra: the job is at a mid-size logistics company; I have 4 years of Python and Airflow experience.

### Model

```
<context>
I am applying for a data engineer position at a mid-size logistics company. I have 4 years of professional experience with Python and Apache Airflow. The purpose of this letter is to present myself as a strong fit for this specific role, not as a generic applicant.
</context>

<material>
[ATTACH: the full job description, including responsibilities and requirements]
[ATTACH: my CV or a short factual summary of my background]
</material>

<task>
Write a complete first-draft cover letter tailored to this specific job posting. The letter must connect my Python and Airflow experience to the concrete needs described in the job description, so that the reader sees a direct fit rather than a generic application.
</task>

<constraints>
- Professional tone, but not stiff or overly formal.
- 250–300 words.
- No clichés such as "I am passionate about," "I am a hard worker," or "I am a team player" without concrete backing.
- Every claim about my background must come from the attached material; do not invent projects, tools, or achievements.
- Do not mention salary, availability, or personal circumstances beyond what is relevant to the role.
</constraints>

<done_when>
- The letter names the position and the company.
- At least two specific points from the job description are explicitly addressed with relevant experience from my background.
- The letter reads as one coherent piece, not a list of bullet points.
- No sentence contains a claim that is not supported by the attached material.
</done_when>

<output_format>
A single ready-to-send cover letter in flowing paragraphs, with a short subject line at the top. No explanations, no bullet points, no alternative versions.
</output_format>

<check_in>
If the job description or my background material is missing, incomplete, or too vague to write a properly tailored letter, stop and ask for the missing information before drafting.
</check_in>
```

- The letter should be in English, matching the language of your request.
- "Mid-size logistics company" is enough context; you do not need to name the company in the prompt itself, but it will appear in the job description you attach.
- The 4 years of Python and Airflow experience is the core selling point to highlight, alongside anything else in your CV that matches the posting.
- You want a single final draft, not multiple options.

**Settings**
- Effort: medium
- Attach: the full job description and your CV (or a short factual summary if you prefer not to share the full CV)
